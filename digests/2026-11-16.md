## Details

### Model Architecture and Efficiency — DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report
S.No. 531 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437

DeepSeek-V3 is a Mixture-of-Experts LM with 671B total parameters, of which 37B are active per token. It reuses Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) and the DeepSeekMoE design from V2. It adds two new pieces: auxiliary-loss-free load balancing and a multi-token-prediction training objective. Pretraining used 14.8T tokens, followed by SFT and RL. The headline efficiency claim is 2.788M H800 GPU-hours for the full training run, with no irrecoverable loss spikes or rollbacks.

**Why it matters:** it reset expectations for how cheaply a frontier-class open-weight model can be trained, and its architecture choices were widely copied. **Caveats:** the GPU-hour figure covers the final training run only, not research or ablations. The "comparable to closed models" claim rests on the authors' own benchmark selection.

### Preference Optimization and RLHF — Is DPO Superior to PPO for LLM Alignment? A Comprehensive Study
S.No. 293 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10719

DPO dominates academic leaderboards, while deployed systems mostly use reward-model + PPO pipelines. This paper studies DPO theoretically and empirically, argues that it has fundamental limitations, and identifies the key factors behind strong PPO performance on LLMs. Across testbeds from dialogue to competitive code, the abstract reports that PPO surpasses the other alignment methods in all cases and reaches state of the art on challenging code competitions.

**Why it matters:** it is a useful counterweight to the assumption that reward-free methods are strictly easier and better, and it gives practical PPO tuning guidance. **Caveats:** the result depends on careful PPO implementation (the code is public as ReaLHF). Newer preference-optimization variants published after April 2024 are not covered.

### Multimodal Models — Autoregressive Model Beats Diffusion: Llama for Scalable Image Generation (LlamaGen)
S.No. 145 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06525

LlamaGen applies vanilla next-token prediction, with no vision-specific inductive biases, to image generation. Its contributions are: an image tokenizer with 16× downsampling (0.94 rFID, 97% codebook usage); class-conditional models from 111M to 3.1B parameters reaching 2.18 FID on ImageNet 256×256, beating LDM and DiT; and a 775M text-to-image model. It also shows that LLM serving stacks give a 326–414% inference speedup.

**Why it matters:** it is evidence that one LLM-style architecture and serving stack can cover image generation, which supports unified multimodal models. **Caveats:** the diffusion baselines are LDM and DiT rather than the strongest diffusion systems of 2024+. The text-to-image model is described only as "competitive."

### Long-Context Learning and Inference — LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens
S.No. 56 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13753

LongRoPE extends pretrained LLMs to a 2048k-token context. It needs at most 1k fine-tuning steps, all at training lengths of 256k or less. It has three parts: (i) an efficient search over two kinds of non-uniformity in positional interpolation, which alone gives 8× extension without fine-tuning; (ii) progressive extension (fine-tune to 256k, then interpolate again to 2048k); (iii) a readjustment at 8k to recover short-context performance. Experiments use LLaMA2 and Mistral.

**Why it matters:** it changes only the positional embedding, so existing architectures and optimizations still apply, and it became a reference method for RoPE scaling (later used in Phi-3 long-context variants). **Caveats:** supporting 2M positions does not mean the model can retrieve and reason well over 2M tokens. The abstract gives no real-task numbers at the full length.

### Reasoning and Knowledge — DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning
S.No. 532 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948

DeepSeek-R1 shows that LLM reasoning can be elicited through pure RL on verifiable tasks, without human-annotated reasoning trajectories. Advanced behaviours such as self-reflection, verification and dynamic strategy adaptation emerge during training. The resulting model beats supervised-on-demonstrations counterparts on math, coding competitions and STEM. Its reasoning traces can also be distilled into smaller models.

**Why it matters:** it was arguably the defining open reasoning-model paper of 2025, and it started the wave of R1-style RL work (several of this repo's #540–#610 papers build on it). **Caveats:** the current abstract (the journal version) gives no concrete benchmark numbers. Gains are concentrated on verifiable domains, and later work in this list (#570) questions whether RL expands capability beyond the base model.

### Quantization and Compression — The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits
S.No. 65 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17764

BitNet b1.58 restricts every weight to ternary values {-1, 0, 1}. The authors report that it matches a full-precision (FP16/BF16) Transformer of the same size and training tokens on both perplexity and end-task performance, while being much cheaper in latency, memory, throughput and energy. They argue it defines a new scaling law and training recipe, and that it motivates dedicated 1-bit hardware.

**Why it matters:** it reframes low-bit models as something you train natively rather than a lossy post-hoc compression step. **Caveats:** it requires training from scratch, so it is not a way to quantize existing checkpoints. The efficiency gains assume kernels and hardware that exploit ternary math, and the abstract does not give the model scales tested.

### Evaluation and Benchmarks — GSM-Symbolic: Understanding the Limitations of Mathematical Reasoning in Large Language Models
S.No. 441 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05229

GSM-Symbolic generates GSM8K-style problems from symbolic templates, which allows controlled variants of the same question. Across many open and closed SOTA models, accuracy varies noticeably between instantiations and drops when only the numbers change. Accuracy also degrades as the number of clauses grows. Adding one seemingly relevant but irrelevant clause causes drops of up to 65%. The authors hypothesize that models replicate reasoning steps from their training data rather than reasoning logically.

**Why it matters:** it is a widely cited warning that static benchmark scores can overstate reasoning ability, and its template method is simple to reuse against contamination. **Caveats:** the "no genuine reasoning" conclusion is presented as a hypothesis and has been debated. The models tested predate most of the RL-trained reasoning models.

### Instruction Tuning and Alignment — The Instruction Hierarchy: Training LLMs to Prioritize Privileged Instructions
S.No. 300 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13208

The paper argues that a root cause of prompt injection and jailbreaks is that LLMs treat developer system prompts with the same priority as untrusted user or third-party text. It defines an explicit hierarchy for resolving conflicting instructions and a synthetic-data method that teaches models to ignore lower-privileged instructions. Applied to GPT-3.5, this greatly improves robustness, including against attack types not seen in training, with minimal loss of general capability.

**Why it matters:** it is a practical, training-based defence that maps onto how agentic and tool-using systems actually mix trusted and untrusted inputs. **Caveats:** results are on one proprietary model, and the abstract does not quantify the robustness gains.

### Surveys and Meta-Analysis — The Prompt Report: A Systematic Survey of Prompting Techniques
S.No. 126 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06608

The paper organizes a fragmented field into a 33-term vocabulary, a taxonomy of 58 text prompting techniques, and 40 techniques for other modalities. It adds best-practice guidance and a meta-analysis of the natural-language prefix-prompting literature, and positions itself as the most comprehensive prompt-engineering survey to date.

**Why it matters:** it is a shared reference vocabulary for practitioners and a good entry point to the literature. **Caveats:** it is a mid-2024 snapshot, so prompting for reasoning models and agent harnesses has moved on since. It is a survey, so it reports no new empirical results beyond the meta-analysis.

### Applications and Tooling — AlphaEvolve: A Coding Agent for Scientific and Algorithmic Discovery
S.No. 568 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131

AlphaEvolve runs an autonomous evolutionary loop in which LLMs propose direct code changes and automated evaluators score them. At Google it produced a more efficient data-centre scheduling algorithm, simplified hardware-accelerator circuit design, and sped up training of its own underlying LLM. In mathematics and CS it found new provably correct algorithms, including a procedure for multiplying 4×4 complex matrices with 48 scalar multiplications. The authors describe this as the first improvement over Strassen in that setting in 56 years.

**Why it matters:** it is a concrete example of LLM agents producing new, verifiable results. **Caveats:** it only applies to problems with reliable automated evaluators. It is a white paper, and the infrastructure results are internal to Google and hard to reproduce independently.
