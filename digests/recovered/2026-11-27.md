# Weekly LLM Papers Digest — 2026-11-27

This is the first digest for this ledger, so every category had candidates. I read the arXiv abstract for all 10 featured papers. Every result below comes from those abstracts; I did not read the full papers.

## TL;DR

- **Model Architecture and Efficiency** — *DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report*: a 671B-total / 37B-active MoE trained in 2.788M H800 GPU-hours that matches leading closed models.
- **Preference Optimization and RLHF** — *SimPO*: drops DPO's reference model by using length-normalized log-probability as the implicit reward, gaining up to +6.4 on AlpacaEval 2 and +7.5 on Arena-Hard over DPO.
- **Multimodal Models** — *Chameleon*: one early-fusion, token-based model that reads and generates any mix of images and text.
- **Long-Context Learning and Inference** — *LongRoPE*: stretches a pre-trained LLM's context to 2048k tokens with ≤1k fine-tuning steps by searching non-uniform RoPE interpolation.
- **Reasoning and Knowledge** — *DeepSeek-R1*: pure RL, without human-labeled reasoning traces, produces self-reflection and verification, and the resulting reasoning can be distilled into smaller models.
- **Quantization and Compression** — *The Era of 1-bit LLMs (BitNet b1.58)*: ternary {-1, 0, 1} weights match FP16/BF16 models of the same size and training tokens at much lower cost.
- **Evaluation and Benchmarks** — *RewardBench*: the first broad benchmark and leaderboard built for evaluating reward models themselves.
- **Instruction Tuning and Alignment** — *Tülu 3*: a fully open post-training recipe (SFT → DPO → RLVR) on Llama 3.1 that beats Llama 3.1 Instruct, Qwen 2.5 Instruct and GPT-4o-mini.
- **Surveys and Meta-Analysis** — *The Prompt Report*: a taxonomy of 58 text prompting techniques and 40 techniques for other modalities, plus a shared vocabulary of 33 terms.
- **Applications and Tooling** — *AlphaEvolve*: an evolutionary LLM coding agent that found new algorithms, including 4×4 complex matrix multiplication with 48 multiplications.

## Details

### Model Architecture and Efficiency — DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report
S.No. 531 · [arXiv:2412.19437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437)

DeepSeek-V3 is a Mixture-of-Experts model with 671B total parameters and 37B active per token. It builds on DeepSeek-V2's Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) and DeepSeekMoE, and adds two things: load balancing without an auxiliary loss, and a multi-token prediction training objective. It was pre-trained on 14.8T tokens, then given SFT and RL. The authors report that it beats other open models and is comparable to leading closed ones. The full training run took only 2.788M H800 GPU-hours, with no irrecoverable loss spikes and no rollbacks.

**Why it matters:** it reset expectations for how cheaply a frontier-class open model can be trained, and its architecture choices (MLA, auxiliary-loss-free balancing, MTP) have been widely copied since. **Caveats:** the GPU-hour figure covers the final training run only. It leaves out research, ablations and data costs, so it is not the total cost of building the model.

### Preference Optimization and RLHF — SimPO: Simple Preference Optimization with a Reference-Free Reward
S.No. 109 · [arXiv:2405.14734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14734)

SimPO replaces DPO's implicit reward, a log-ratio against a reference model, with the sequence's *average* log-probability. It also adds a target reward margin to the Bradley-Terry objective. Removing the reference model saves compute and memory, and the authors argue the new reward better matches how the model actually generates text. Across Mistral, Llama 3 and Gemma 2 setups it beats DPO by up to 6.4 points on AlpacaEval 2 and up to 7.5 on Arena-Hard, without making responses much longer. Their best Gemma-2-9B-it model reaches a 72.4% length-controlled win rate on AlpacaEval 2.

**Why it matters:** it is a drop-in DPO replacement that is cheaper to run and has become a standard baseline. **Caveats:** the evaluations rely mostly on LLM-judged chat benchmarks. Gains on reasoning or other downstream tasks are not covered in the abstract.

### Multimodal Models — Chameleon: Mixed-Modal Early-Fusion Foundation Models
S.No. 123 · [arXiv:2405.09818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09818)

Chameleon turns images and text into a single token stream and trains one model on it from scratch ("early fusion"). It can therefore understand and generate interleaved image-text documents in any order. The paper gives a stable training recipe, an alignment recipe and an architecture tuned for this setting. The authors report state-of-the-art image captioning, better text-only results than Llama-2, results competitive with Mixtral 8x7B and Gemini-Pro, and non-trivial image generation. In human evaluations of long-form mixed-modal generation, it matches or beats Gemini Pro and GPT-4V.

**Why it matters:** it is a key reference design for "any-to-any" native multimodal models, as opposed to bolting a vision encoder onto an LLM. **Caveats:** the strongest mixed-modal claims rest on a new human evaluation that the authors designed themselves.

### Long-Context Learning and Inference — LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens
S.No. 56 · [arXiv:2402.13753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13753)

LongRoPE extends a pre-trained LLM's context window to 2048k tokens, using at most about 1k fine-tuning steps on sequences up to 256k. It does this in three steps:
1. It searches for non-uniform positional-interpolation factors. This alone gives an 8× extension without fine-tuning.
2. It extends progressively: first fine-tune to 256k, then interpolate a second time to reach 2048k.
3. It re-adjusts on 8k sequences to recover short-context performance.

Only the positional embedding changes, so existing inference optimizations still work. Experiments use LLaMA2 and Mistral.

**Why it matters:** it showed RoPE scaling could reach millions of tokens cheaply, and the technique was used in Phi-3's long-context variants. **Caveats:** a model that accepts 2M tokens does not necessarily *use* them well. Later benchmarks show long-context reasoning quality drops off well before the nominal window.

### Reasoning and Knowledge — DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning
S.No. 532 · [arXiv:2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948)

DeepSeek-R1 shows that pure RL on verifiable tasks can produce strong reasoning without human-annotated reasoning trajectories. Behaviors such as self-reflection, verification and adapting strategy mid-solution emerge on their own. The trained model beats counterparts trained with supervised learning on human demonstrations in math, competitive coding and STEM. The authors also show these reasoning patterns can be distilled into smaller models.

**Why it matters:** it is arguably the paper that started the open RLVR / "reasoning model" wave, and much of this repo's 2025 list builds on it. **Caveats:** the abstract describes verifiable domains. Many follow-ups (e.g., S.No. 570, 581) question how much RL adds beyond what the base model can already do.

### Quantization and Compression — The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits
S.No. 65 · [arXiv:2402.17764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17764)

BitNet b1.58 restricts every weight to the ternary set {-1, 0, 1}, which carries about 1.58 bits per weight. The authors report that it matches a full-precision (FP16/BF16) Transformer of the same size and training-token budget in both perplexity and end-task performance, while being much cheaper in latency, memory, throughput and energy. They present this as a new scaling recipe and a reason to build hardware specialized for 1-bit models.

**Why it matters:** it reframes low-bit models from post-hoc compression to native low-precision training, and it set off a line of follow-ups (e.g., BitNet a4.8, S.No. 495). **Caveats:** it is a short paper, and the models must be trained from scratch, so it does not compress existing checkpoints. The efficiency gains also depend on kernels and hardware that were not widely available when it was published.

### Evaluation and Benchmarks — RewardBench: Evaluating Reward Models for Language Modeling
S.No. 242 · [arXiv:2403.13787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13787)

RewardBench is a dataset and codebase of prompt / chosen / rejected triples covering chat, reasoning and safety. Many pairs differ in subtle but checkable ways, such as a bug or a factual error. On its leaderboard it evaluates classifier reward models alongside DPO's implicit rewards. Findings include tendencies to over-refuse, weaknesses in reasoning, and shortcomings in instruction following.

**Why it matters:** reward models drive RLHF but were rarely evaluated directly, and RewardBench became the default yardstick for them. **Caveats:** accuracy on preference pairs does not necessarily predict how much a reward model improves the policy trained against it.

### Instruction Tuning and Alignment — Tülu 3: Pushing Frontiers in Open Language Model Post-Training
S.No. 515 · [arXiv:2411.15124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124)

Tülu 3 is a family of post-trained Llama 3.1 models released fully open: data, code, recipes and evaluation. The pipeline runs SFT, then DPO, then a new stage the authors call Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The authors report it beats the instruct versions of Llama 3.1, Qwen 2.5 and Mistral, and also closed models such as GPT-4o-mini and Claude 3.5-Haiku. It also contributes a decontaminated multi-task evaluation suite, split into development and unseen sets, and documents methods that did *not* reliably help.

**Why it matters:** it is the most complete open post-training reference available, and it coined "RLVR". **Caveats:** the comparisons are at the time of release and depend on the authors' chosen evaluation suite.

### Surveys and Meta-Analysis — The Prompt Report: A Systematic Survey of Prompting Techniques
S.No. 126 · [arXiv:2406.06608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06608)

This survey organizes the fragmented prompting literature. It proposes a vocabulary of 33 terms, a taxonomy of 58 text prompting techniques plus 40 for other modalities, practical guidelines for prompting state-of-the-art LLMs, and a meta-analysis of the natural-language prefix-prompting literature.

**Why it matters:** it gives the field a shared vocabulary and works as a single reference for prompting methods. **Caveats:** it is a survey with no new modeling results, and prompting best practices change quickly as models change.

### Applications and Tooling — AlphaEvolve: A Coding Agent for Scientific and Algorithmic Discovery
S.No. 568 · [arXiv:2506.13131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131)

AlphaEvolve runs a set of LLMs in an evolutionary loop that edits code directly and is scored by automated evaluators. Inside Google it found:
- a more efficient data-center scheduling algorithm,
- a simplification of a hardware-accelerator circuit,
- speedups to the training of the LLM that powers AlphaEvolve itself.

It also discovered provably correct algorithms that beat the state of the art on several math and computer-science problems. One example is multiplying two 4×4 complex matrices with 48 scalar multiplications, the first improvement over Strassen's algorithm in this setting in 56 years.

**Why it matters:** it is a concrete case of LLM agents producing verifiable new scientific and engineering results, not just benchmark scores. **Caveats:** the method needs problems with a reliable automated evaluator. It is a white paper, and the system is not openly available.
